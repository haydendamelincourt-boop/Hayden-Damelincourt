How I got here

Four interests, one direction

The ocean, soccer, entrepreneurship, and travel aren't separate from finance for me — they're where I learned to work under pressure, take a loss, and build something from nothing. Each one shows up in how I approach a deal.

I'm a Finance and History double major at Florida State University, a research assistant, and a member of Noles on Wall Street. My goal: start my own private equity firm.

What shaped me

The interests behind the résumé

01

The Ocean

I scuba dive, free dive, and spent a summer helping save sea turtles on my local Florida beaches. Diving that often means watching climate change damage reefs and nesting grounds firsthand, year over year.

That's what led me to Sailplan, where I worked with clients including the U.S. Navy, U.S. Army, and Disney Cruise Line to cut their emissions footprint. I learned fundraising and business development, but more importantly I saw the work move the needle in my local waters — which is why I'm serious about investing in renewable energy and climate tech.

02

Soccer

I've played soccer my whole life, up through the semi-professional level. It taught me how to lose, work hard, and function inside a team — and it's where I first fell for a competitive atmosphere.

Private equity has that same atmosphere. Deals fall through, models are wrong, competitions don't go your way. Soccer trained me to take the loss, learn from it, and show up again — which is the job.

03

Entrepreneurship

In high school, I co-founded myKickstrt with two other students. We raised over $10,000, drew interest from major Florida universities, and became part of a wider program promoting high school entrepreneurship across North America. myKickstrt connected students at underprivileged high schools with internships, jobs, and community service — real résumé lines that closed the disparity gap in college applications.

Freshman year at FSU, I worked with CollegeTown AI, centralizing student-facing social, educational, and local business content into one platform. Building both is what convinced me I want to build something bigger long-term: my own private equity firm.

04

Travel

I've traveled since I was a kid — Canada, the U.S., Costa Rica, France. I look for adventure: hiking, skiing, the cliff worth jumping off. I've also worked summers in local hotels, seeing hospitality from the operating side, not just as a guest.

Between the travel and the front desk, I understand both what makes a stay worth paying for and what it costs to run the building behind it — which is why hospitality investing interests me.

Different rooms, same lesson: take the hit, stay curious, build instead of wait. Here's where I've put that to work.

Buy-side · Platform acquisition

Project Alpha

A buy-side thesis on a full-service Mechanical, Plumbing & Electrical (MP&E) contractor with a 50+ year operating history across the Southeast. We built the case around three pillars: strategic M&A into a highly fragmented market, operational centralization to unlock $3–4M in cost synergies, and organic growth from a wave of infrastructure and data center investment across the region.

Sell-side · Public REIT

Sunstone Hotel Investors

A sell-side thesis on a publicly traded lodging REIT (NYSE: SHO) trading at a 43% discount to NAV amid rising activist pressure and declining profitability. We recommended a sale to a financial sponsor with hospitality specialization, arguing the market was mispricing a portfolio of luxury and upper-upscale assets that private buyers would value far closer to replacement cost.

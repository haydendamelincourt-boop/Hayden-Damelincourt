A bit about how I got here

Four interests, one thread

Everything on this page relays back to one idea. What I care about outside of finance are the same things that made me want to be in finance. The ocean, soccer, entrepreneurship, and travel didn't just fill my free time, they taught me lessons I now recognize every time I'm underwriting a deal, sitting in a competition room, or working with a team under pressure.

I'm a Finance and History major at Florida State University, an Research Assistant, and a member of the Noles on Wall Street Organization. My long-term goal is to start my own private equity firm. Everything below is the case for why.

What shaped me

The interests behind the résumé

01

The Ocean

I scuba dive, free dive, and spent a summer helping save sea turtles on my local Florida beaches. Being in the water that often means watching first-hand how climate change and greenhouse gases affect marine ecosystems. Every year the water clarity, reefs, and nesting spots are being destroyed.

That's what pulled me toward Sailplan, where I worked with clients including the U.S. Navy, U.S. Army, and Disney Cruise Line to help reduce their emissions footprint. I didn't just learn fundraising and business development there, I got to see the work actually move the needle in my local waters. It's why I'm genuinely passionate about investing in renewable energy and climate tech, not just because the numbers work, but because I've seen the problem up close.

02

Soccer

I've played soccer my entire life, all the way up through the semi-proffesional level. It taught me about failure and about the value of hard work and working inside a team environment. More than anything, it made me love a competitive atmosphere.

I find that same atmosphere in private equity. Finance is an industry full of setbacks: deals fall through, models are wrong, competitions don't go your way. Soccer taught me to stay open to learning and to keep showing up after a loss, which is exactly the mindset this industry demands.

03

Entrepreneurship

I've always been drawn to building things. My favorite movie growing up was Star Wars, and I was the kid obsessed with imagining entire worlds that didn't exist yet. That same creativity is what eventually pulled me into starting things for real.

In high school, I co-founded myKickstrt with two other students. We raised over $10,000, earned interest from major Florida universities, and represented a wider program encouraging high school entrepreneurship across North America. myKickstrt connected students at underprivileged high schools with internship, job, and community service opportunities. The goal was to help these students build real résumés and close the disparity gap in college applications.

During my freshman year at FSU, I worked with CollegeTown AI, a startup built to centralize student-facing social, educational, and local business content into one place. It was through these ventures that I decided I wanted to build something of my own long-term. The solution, a private equity firm.

04

Travel

I've loved to travel my entire life. I've visited Canada, the U.S., Costa Rica, France, and more. I'm drawn to adventure trips, hiking with friends, skiing, finding the cliff worth jumping off of. In addition, during my summers, I work in local hotels. This gave me a look at the hospitality industry from an operational perspective, not just as a guest.

Between the travel itself and the time behind the front desk, I came away genuinely interested in investing within hospitality. I understand both what makes a stay worth paying for and what it actually costs to run the building behind it.

Put together, these four interests point in the same direction. I'm drawn to industries I can see and touch. I don't back down from setbacks. I like building things from nothing. And I'd rather learn a market by living in it than by reading about it. That's the lens I bring to private equity, and to every case, deck, and deal below.

Buy-side · Platform acquisition

Project Alpha

A buy-side thesis on a full-service Mechanical, Plumbing & Electrical (MP&E) contractor with a 50+ year operating history across the Southeast. We built the case around three pillars: strategic M&A into a highly fragmented market, operational centralization to unlock $3–4M in cost synergies, and organic growth from a wave of infrastructure and data center investment across the region.

Sell-side · Public REIT

Sunstone Hotel Investors

A sell-side thesis on a publicly traded lodging REIT (NYSE: SHO) trading at a 43% discount to NAV amid rising activist pressure and declining profitability. We recommended a sale to a financial sponsor with hospitality specialization, arguing the market was mispricing a portfolio of luxury and upper-upscale assets that private buyers would value far closer to replacement cost.

What I'd bring to Recalc

Community, mentorship, and where this goes next

Outside of casework, I try to make a real difference in the communities I'm part of. I'm a mentor with FSU's Investment Banking Association, where I get to do for other students what I once needed myself — a straight answer, a second look at a resume, someone a year or two ahead willing to spend the time.

That combination — genuine curiosity about markets, a track record of showing up on teams that win and teams that lose, and a habit of building things instead of waiting for them to be built — is what I'd bring to the Recalc community. I'm not finished. I'm a sophomore with a long runway and a clear goal: build the judgment now to run my own fund later.
